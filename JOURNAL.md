# November 18th: Initial schematic!
![schematic](https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a1f092-0444-752e-a148-d0fdd42def7d/2026-11-18_13-29-59.png)
Today (and a little bit yesterday), I somehow managed to learn how to use KiCad to sketch a schematic without much struggling. It's a pretty simple circuit, but I'm still proud of it. I also found all of my parts, and I'm getting them all on Digikey to save on shipping. (which has a $5 shipping option but it's kind of hidden and took me a while to find it). I was originally going to make it battery powered but I decided against doing that because a) it would add more cost and b) I'd rather just have it be plugged in than have one more thing to need to change the battery and forget about.
**Time spent: 2h**

# November 19th: A bit of PCB
Today I opened up KiCad and was mildly annoyed because the potentiometer I had chosen didn't have a model in KiCad's libraries. *Only a minor setback*, I thought. *I'll just make one from the datasheet*. 
Well guess who decided to unhelpfully not put the pin labeling on the datasheet? These guys!!!!
Long story short I got too scared that I would wire the PCB wrong and switched potentiometers. This one also has a KiCad component that I can just download! Yay!!!!
Anyway now I have PCB footprints for all of the components, some built in to KiCad and some from the internet.
**Time spent: 0.5h**

# November 20th: More PCB
After getting all of the footprints found and assigned, I clicked the add parts to PCB button and was met with a mess of scattered parts and connections between them. Making the actual traces was super quick once I had everything arranged, but the arranging took *soooo* long. I probably went through three or four different layouts before I settled on one I liked. It's nice and compact and is layed out in a way that I think is logical. 
**Time spent: 1.5h**
